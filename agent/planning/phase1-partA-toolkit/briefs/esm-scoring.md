# ESM2 / ESM-C embeddings & likelihood scoring, ESM-IF inverse folding

**One-line identity.** Protein language model tooling used for three distinct purposes — zero-shot mutation/fitness scoring via masked-marginal pseudo-likelihood (ESM2, ESM-C), sequence embeddings for similarity/diversity monitoring (ESM2, ESM-C), and structure-conditioned sequence design as an alternative inverse-folder (ESM-IF). **Scope note:** ESMFold (the folding model built on the same ESM2 backbone) is covered separately in `esmfold.md`; this brief stays off structure prediction entirely.

Like `esmfold.md`, none of ESM2, ESM-C, or ESM-IF appear in `impress-a-refcodes/tools/` (confirmed: no `esm`, `esm2`, `esm3`, or `evolutionaryscale` directory under `tools/`). This brief is WebSearch-derived from upstream repos, papers, and license pages.

## Identity
- **Version / release examined:** **ESM2** — `facebookresearch/esm` (`fair-esm` on PyPI), model family `esm2_t*_*M_UR50D` at six named sizes (8M, 35M, 150M, 650M, 3B, 15B parameters); zero-shot variant-scoring pattern documented in the repo's `examples/variant-prediction/predict.py`. **ESM-IF1** — same `facebookresearch/esm` repo, `esm.inverse_folding` module, `esm_if1_gvp4_t16_142M_UR50` checkpoint (GVP-GNN structure encoder + autoregressive transformer decoder). **ESM-C** — a separate org and codebase, `evolutionaryscale/esm` on GitHub, released at 300M and 600M ("Open" tiers) plus larger (up to 6B-class per public reporting) served only via the hosted **Forge API**.
- **Provenance:** ESM2 and ESM-IF1 — Meta AI (FAIR). **MIT-licensed** (confirmed for ESMFold's identical repo in `esmfold.md`; applies to ESM2/ESM-IF1 as the same repository). ESM-C — **EvolutionaryScale**, a separate company spun out from the original ESM lineage; **not MIT**, governed by the "Cambrian" license family (see Licensing below).
- **Maturity:** ESM2/ESM-IF1 — production/mature, effectively frozen in the same sense `esmfold.md` describes for ESMFold (same repo, same release cadence, superseded in Meta's own active work but stable and widely used as a baseline). ESM-C — actively developed, newer (2024–2026), positioned by EvolutionaryScale as the maintained successor line; per `esmfold.md`, a 2026 "ESMFold2" effort combining a 6B-parameter ESM-C with a diffusion structure head was found in search but is out of scope for both this brief and `esmfold.md`.

## Scientific role
Three distinct pipeline stages, all under the **score** / **analyze** / **inverse-fold** headings, serving primarily the **stability/thermostabilization** problem class (zero-shot scoring) with a general-purpose supporting role across all four classes (embeddings for diversity monitoring; ESM-IF for any backbone needing a sequence).

### Pseudo-likelihood / masked-marginal scoring — zero-shot fitness and stability proxy
For a candidate mutation, mask the mutated position(s), compute the model's log-probability for the wild-type and mutant residue under the masked-language-model objective, and score the mutation as the log-probability difference (the **masked-marginal** method; two cheaper alternatives — **wild-type marginal** and **pseudo-perplexity (PPPL)** — exist but masked-marginal is reported to match or beat wild-type-marginal on every benchmarked assay). This requires **no experimental data and no structure** — it is a pure sequence-evolutionary signal, which is both its strength (zero marginal cost, applicable to any sequence) and its central limitation.

**What it does and does not predict well — stated precisely, since this is the known failure mode the agent must respect:** ESM2's likelihood reflects the model's estimate of how consistent a sequence is with the patterns of natural protein evolution — a proxy for **fitness-in-context** (would this sequence plausibly have arisen and been selected for in nature) — which is *correlated with* but *not the same quantity as* thermodynamic folding stability (ΔΔG). The correlation is real and useful for triage but is not calibrated the way a ΔΔG predictor's output is. Two specific, sourced failure conditions:
- **De novo designed proteins have no evolutionary history to be scored against.** A sequence generated by RFdiffusion3 + ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN has never been under natural selection, so ESM2's "does this look like something evolution would produce" signal is answering a question that doesn't straightforwardly map onto "is this stable/functional." The comparative literature on this exact scenario is stark: in large-scale evaluation of de novo miniprotein designs, confidence metrics from this same model family showed **minimal discrimination between experimentally stable and unstable designs** — high model confidence did not track real success. Treat ESM2 pseudo-likelihood on a de novo design as **weakly informative at best, actively misleading at worst**, and never as a stand-alone gate for anything downstream of an RFdiffusion-class generator.
- **Disordered regions and highly divergent families degrade the signal.** Pseudo-log-likelihood correlates less with experimental deep-mutational-scanning (DMS) fitness scores in disordered regions, and zero-shot predictions are documented as inconsistent for highly divergent protein families — a category that structurally includes computationally designed sequences that deliberately diverge from natural sequence space.

### Embeddings for similarity, clustering, and diversity assessment
Per-residue hidden states from any transformer layer (commonly the final layer, e.g. layer 33 of ESM2-650M), mean-pooled over sequence length, give a fixed-size vector per sequence usable for cosine similarity, clustering, or dimensionality reduction (PCA/UMAP). This is the concrete tool for a specific autonomous-agent watchdog need named in this project's shared context: **tracking whether a design population is collapsing to a single mode.** An agent running many parallel RFdiffusion/MPNN trajectories can embed each candidate cheaply (the forward pass is already needed for scoring, so the embedding is close to free as a byproduct) and monitor the population's embedding-space spread iteration over iteration — a shrinking spread is a direct, quantitative signal of mode collapse that doesn't require any structural comparison tool (FoldSeek, US-align) to be invoked at all.

### ESM-IF as an inverse-folding alternative to ProteinMPNN
ESM-IF1 takes a backbone structure and autoregressively designs a sequence conditioned on it — the same **inverse-fold** stage ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN occupy (Core tool, per `proteinmpnn-ligandmpnn.md`), but trained differently: ESM-IF1 was trained on ~12M **AlphaFold2-predicted structures** (a self-distillation training set), whereas ProteinMPNN trained on experimental PDB structures directly. Head-to-head comparisons in the literature give ProteinMPNN a modest edge on structural-fidelity metrics of its own designs (TM-score 0.84±0.03, mean pLDDT 0.79±0.03) over ESM-IF1 (TM-score 0.81±0.01, mean pLDDT 0.72±0.04), and a much larger gap opens on specialized tasks: for antibody CDR-H3 loop design specifically, ESM-IF1 achieves only **43% amino-acid recovery**, well below both AbMPNN (ProteinMPNN-derived, 56%) and task-specialized AntiFold (60%). **Recommendation: ESM-IF1 is not a replacement for ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN in this toolkit** — it is worth keeping as a **secondary inverse-folder for ensembling or independent cross-validation** of an MPNN-designed sequence, precisely because its different training distribution (AF2-predicted structures vs. experimental PDB) makes its errors less correlated with MPNN's own, which is useful when an agent wants a second, differently-biased opinion on whether a backbone is well-designable at all — not for routine primary sequence design.

## Invocation & I/O contract
- **How a unit of work is invoked (ESM2 scoring):**
  ```python
  import esm, torch
  model, alphabet = esm.pretrained.esm2_t33_650M_UR50D()
  model = model.eval().cuda()
  batch_converter = alphabet.get_batch_converter()
  # masked-marginal: for each mutated position, mask it, take log-softmax logits,
  # score = logp(mutant_aa) - logp(wt_aa), summed over all mutated positions
  ```
  Pattern documented in `facebookresearch/esm`'s `examples/variant-prediction/predict.py`; the Hugging Face `transformers` route (`EsmForMaskedLM.from_pretrained("facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D")`) is the common alternative.
- **How a unit of work is invoked (embeddings):**
  ```python
  out = model(tokens, repr_layers=[33], return_contacts=False)
  emb = out["representations"][33].mean(1)  # mean-pool over sequence length
  ```
- **How a unit of work is invoked (ESM-IF1):**
  ```python
  import esm.inverse_folding
  model, alphabet = esm.pretrained.esm_if1_gvp4_t16_142M_UR50()
  coords, seq = esm.inverse_folding.util.load_structure(pdb_path)
  sampled_seq = esm.inverse_folding.util.sample_sequence_design(
      model, coords, temperature=1.0)
  ```
- **Inputs:** ESM2 scoring/embeddings — a protein sequence string (or list) plus, for scoring, a mutation list. ESM-IF1 — a backbone structure (PDB/CIF), optionally a target-chain selector.
- **Outputs:** ESM2 scoring — a scalar log-probability-difference score per mutation (higher = more favorable under the model). Embeddings — a fixed-size vector per sequence (dimension = model hidden size, e.g. 1280 for the 650M model). ESM-IF1 — a designed sequence string, or (via the lower-level `get_sequence_loss` API) a per-residue negative log-likelihood usable as a design-confidence proxy.
- **A concrete example:** the patterns above are the documented usage from the `fair-esm` repo README and `examples/variant-prediction/`; no repo-local example exists since neither tool is vendored in `impress-a-refcodes/`.

## Compute pattern
- **Pattern:** **P1** (GPU-node-local, in-job) for all self-hosted ESM2/ESM-IF1 use. **P5** (network service over HTTPS) when using EvolutionaryScale's hosted **Forge API** for ESM-C rather than self-hosting weights.
- **GPU vendor portability:** plausible-portable, unverified — same reasoning as `esmfold.md`: pure PyTorch, no custom CUDA kernels found in `fair-esm` for ESM2/ESM-IF1. ESM-C's codebase (`evolutionaryscale/esm`) was not independently checked for CUDA-only kernels in this search; treat separately and unverified.
- **State model:** stateless per call for all three use cases.
- **Data locality:** self-contained; only model weights need local caching (ESM2) or no local staging at all (ESM-C via Forge API).
- **Staging burden:** model weights only, no sequence/structure database — sizes scale with the chosen checkpoint (see size table below); this is the smallest staging burden of any GPU tool in the toolkit alongside ESMFold.
- **Container availability:** community at best; no official first-party container confirmed for any of the three in this search.

### Model sizes and VRAM — pick a size that fits alongside other GPU work
| Model | Parameters | Approx. weights (bf16) | Approx. inference VRAM (typical sequence lengths) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ESM2-8M | 8M | <0.1 GB | <1 GB | trivial, CPU-viable, useful only for the very cheapest screening pass |
| ESM2-35M | 35M | ~0.1 GB | ~1 GB | still CPU-viable |
| ESM2-150M | 150M | ~0.3 GB | ~1–2 GB | good low-cost default for embeddings/diversity monitoring |
| ESM2-650M | 650M | ~1.3 GB | **~4 GB** (confirmed figure, typical sequences) | the commonly-used default for zero-shot scoring; fits comfortably alongside other P1 GPU work on any target platform |
| ESM2-3B | 3B | ~6 GB | **~12–16 GB** (confirmed figure, antibody-length ~120-residue sequences; scales up with length) | meaningful accuracy step up; needs a real fraction of a modern GPU's memory, plan allocation accordingly |
| ESM2-15B | 15B | ~30 GB (bf16) | **~40–80 GB+, reasoned extrapolation, not independently confirmed** — realistically needs a full high-memory GPU (A100/H100-class 80GB) or model sharding | largest ESM2 size; likely not worth co-locating with other P1 GPU work in the same job — budget it as a dedicated slice if used at all |
| ESM-C 300M / 600M | 300M / 600M | comparable to ESM2-150M/650M-class | comparable order of magnitude, not independently confirmed | "Open" self-hostable tiers, see licensing below |

The practical guidance for an agent choosing a checkpoint: **650M is the sweet spot** for zero-shot scoring — small enough to co-locate with P1 folding/design work on the same GPU, large enough to be the size most benchmarking literature reports figures for. Reserve 3B/15B for cases where accuracy matters more than throughput and a dedicated GPU slice is available; use 8M/35M/150M only for the diversity-monitoring embedding use case where raw representational quality matters less than call frequency and cost.

## Deployment on DOE & ACCESS
- **Polaris / ACCESS (CUDA):** straightforward, standard `pip install fair-esm` or `transformers`, same low-friction story as `esmfold.md`.
- **Frontier (AMD MI250X) / Aurora (Intel PVC):** unverified but structurally low-risk for ESM2/ESM-IF1, by the same pure-PyTorch argument `esmfold.md` makes for ESMFold — this is literally the same repository family. ESM-C's portability on ROCm/XPU is **separately unverified** since it is a distinct codebase from a different org; do not assume it inherits ESM2's portability story without checking.
- **Module/conda/apptainer:** plain pip install for ESM2/ESM-IF1, no license gate. ESM-C self-hosted (300M/600M "Open" tiers) likewise installs via pip from `evolutionaryscale/esm`, but carries the license obligations below regardless of how easy the install is.

### Licensing — precise, because it gates whether we can depend on ESM-C at all
- **ESM2 and ESM-IF1: MIT.** No restriction on commercial use, redistribution, or self-hosting. This is the safe, load-bearing default for anything the toolkit depends on structurally.
- **ESM-C: not MIT, and materially more restrictive, with tier-dependent terms:**
  - **ESM-C 600M "Open"** is licensed under the **Cambrian Non-Commercial License Agreement** — non-commercial use only, by individuals or non-commercial organizations; explicitly **prohibits** "any activities by, on behalf of or for a commercial entity" and prohibits building a product/service such as "hosting the AI Model behind an API." It also imposes attribution requirements (must include "ESM" in derivative-work titles, must display "Built with ESM" on any interface incorporating it).
  - **ESM-C 300M "Open"** is licensed under the separate, distinct **Cambrian Open License Agreement** — reported as more permissive, but still **not MIT** and not independently confirmed in this search to permit unrestricted commercial self-hosting; treat as license-gated pending a direct read of that specific agreement's text before depending on it.
  - **Commercial use of any ESM-C tier** — via the hosted API, EvolutionaryScale Forge, or AWS SageMaker — requires a separate **Cambrian Commercial Interface Clickthrough License Agreement.**
  - **Practical implication for this project:** whether IMPRESS-A's operation on DOE/NSF facility allocations counts as "non-commercial" under the Cambrian Non-Commercial terms is a real, non-obvious question — a national-lab or NSF-ACCESS facility potentially serving PI allocations with mixed academic/industry funding could plausibly be read either way, and "hosting the AI Model behind an API" is close to a literal description of what a shared HPC toolkit service does. **This is a legal determination, not an engineering one, and should be resolved explicitly with EvolutionaryScale or project counsel before self-hosting ESM-C 600M in production** — do not assume the non-commercial grant applies by default.

## Agentic surface
- **Native MCP:** community/unverified for both families. The same search result cited in `esmfold.md` — `zeinab-sheikhi/mcp-alphafold` reportedly covering "ESM-2/ESMC embeddings, mutation scoring, landscape scans, and ESMFold structure prediction" — is the only lead found; its scope, maintenance status, and whether it targets self-hosted weights vs. the Forge API were not independently confirmed. Mark **community / unverified**, same caveat level as in `esmfold.md`.
- **Parameters worth exposing for autonomous variation:**

| Parameter | Type | Sane range | Default | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model checkpoint size | enum | 8M–15B (ESM2) / 300M–600M (ESM-C Open) | 650M for scoring | accuracy vs. VRAM/throughput, see size table above |
| scoring method | enum | `masked-marginal` / `wt-marginal` / `pseudo-perplexity` | `masked-marginal` | masked-marginal matches-or-beats wt-marginal on every benchmarked assay but costs one forward pass per masked position rather than one pass total — more expensive, more reliable |
| `repr_layer` (embeddings) | int | 0–(total layers) | final layer | which representation depth to pool; deeper layers generally more semantically meaningful, but must match the loaded model's own layer count |
| ESM-IF1 `temperature` | float | 0.1–1.5 | 1.0 | higher → more sequence diversity, lower per-residue confidence, same trade-off shape as ProteinMPNN's `temperature` |
| embedding pooling window (whole-sequence vs. per-domain) | selector | task-defined | whole-sequence mean-pool | affects what "similarity" means for the diversity-monitoring use case — per-domain pooling is more sensitive to local mode collapse, whole-sequence to global |

- **Parameters that must NOT be agent-varied:** **never compare or rank raw scores computed from different model checkpoints or different scoring methods in the same list** — score scale and calibration differ across model size and across masked-marginal/wt-marginal/PPPL, so mixing them silently produces a meaningless ranking rather than an error. Pin one checkpoint and one scoring method per campaign/comparison.

## Failure modes & what the agent must check
- **Loud failure:** out-of-memory on very long sequences or oversized batch × masked-marginal position count (masked-marginal cost scales with sequence length since it needs one forward pass per position, not per candidate substitution); malformed sequence characters; `repr_layer` index out of range for the loaded checkpoint (raises rather than silently returning garbage).
- **Silent bad output — the central risk, stated in the Scientific role section above and repeated here as the specific check:** a high ESM2 pseudo-likelihood score on a **de novo designed sequence** (any RFdiffusion3/RFD3NA output post-MPNN) must not be treated as evidence of stability or function. The agent must cross-check with a structure-grounded signal (ThermoMPNN, Stability Oracle, or Rosetta `cartesian_ddg` — see `ddg-predictors.md`) before acting on an ESM2-only ranking for anything downstream of a generative structure model. This is not a hypothetical caveat — it is the literature-documented reason confident language-model scores failed to discriminate stable from unstable de novo miniproteins in the study cited above.
- **Checkpoint/method mismatch producing a valid-looking but wrong-scale number:** verify the `repr_layer` argument matches the loaded model's actual depth, and verify the scoring method used is logged alongside every score so a later comparison doesn't silently mix incompatible scales.
- **ESM-IF1 on loop/CDR-heavy targets:** expect a real, documented accuracy gap vs. specialized inverse-folders (43% AAR on CDR-H3 vs. 56–60% for MPNN-derived/specialized alternatives) — do not use ESM-IF1 as the primary designer for antibody-loop-style targets; it is appropriate as a secondary cross-check there, not a primary.
- **Diversity-monitoring embeddings:** this is a watchdog signal on the agent's own design population, not a correctness check of the ESM model itself — a shrinking embedding-space spread across iterations should trigger an agent-level response (increase sampling temperature upstream, diversify seed structures), not a tool-level failure report.

## Cost per unit of work
- **ESM2 scoring/embeddings (650M):** sub-second to ~1 second per sequence on any modern GPU for a single forward pass (embeddings, wt-marginal); a full masked-marginal scan of an N-residue protein costs on the order of N forward passes (one per masked position) rather than one per candidate substitution — a useful property, since scoring all 19 substitutions at a position is free once that position's masked pass has run. Effectively free at campaign scale, same order-of-magnitude cost class as ThermoMPNN (`ddg-predictors.md`).
- **ESM2-3B/15B:** higher per-call cost but still squarely P1, seconds-scale, not P4 material.
- **ESM-IF1:** one structure-conditioned forward pass per sampled sequence, seconds-scale, comparable to ProteinMPNN's own per-sequence cost (`proteinmpnn-ligandmpnn.md`).
- **ESM-C via Forge API:** P5 network-call cost model instead of local GPU-hours — no local resource consumed, but subject to the same latency/quota/outage failure modes as any hosted API (see `llm-oracle.md` for the general P5 resilience posture, directly applicable here).
- **Checkpointable:** n/a — sub-minute P1 calls should never be individually scheduled/checkpointed, per the taxonomy's P6-adjacent guidance for cheap in-job work.

## Verdict
**ESM2 masked-marginal scoring: Recommended.** Cheap, MIT-licensed, complementary sequence-only signal with no marginal cost worth worrying about — genuinely useful for broad triage and, via embeddings, for population-diversity monitoring that no other tool in this toolkit directly provides. It must never be the sole gate for stability or fitness given its distinct-from-ΔΔG semantics and its documented, literature-confirmed blind spot on de novo designed sequences with no evolutionary history.

**ESM-IF1: Recommended, as a secondary/ensemble inverse-folder, not a ProteinMPNN replacement.** ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN remain Core (`proteinmpnn-ligandmpnn.md`) as the toolkit's primary sequence designer; ESM-IF1's differently-sourced training distribution (AF2-predicted structures vs. experimental PDB) makes it a useful, cheap second opinion for cross-validating an MPNN-designed sequence or flagging backbones that are hard to design for, but its own accuracy trails MPNN-family tools on both general structural-fidelity metrics and specialized loop-design tasks.

**ESM-C / ESM3 (EvolutionaryScale family): Defer, pending explicit license review.** The larger-model capability upside is real per EvolutionaryScale's own benchmarking claims, but the Cambrian Non-Commercial License's restrictions — particularly the "hosting behind an API" prohibition and the ambiguity of whether a DOE/NSF facility deployment counts as non-commercial — are not resolved by anything found in this search and should not be assumed favorable. Default to the MIT-licensed ESM2/ESM-IF1 line as the toolkit's load-bearing choice; revisit ESM-C only after a specific legal determination for this project's operating model.
