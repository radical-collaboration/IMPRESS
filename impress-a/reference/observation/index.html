    Observation: what a caller is told, and when¶

    resolution.md runs one direction — toolkit, goal, budget and parameters become specified targets and a programmed Stage. This page runs the other: how the state of a running campaign becomes a CampaignObservation, who is shown it, what of it survives to disk, and by what mechanism any of it reaches a caller outside this process.

    Every step says what it takes, who owns it, what it produces and what refuses it, in the order a cycle executes them. Anything true of how a decision is made from an observation is in control-models.md and is not repeated here. If this page and the code disagree, the code is right and this page is a bug.

    Two things to read it for. Wiring IMPRESS-A beneath an enclosing campaign manager, which needs to know the cadence, the shape and the cost of being told — see decision 0014. And changing any of this safely, for which the thing to preserve is named in each step: the observation is assembled inside a window where the executor is not mutating anything, and almost every rule below exists to keep that window shut.

       absorb a run ────────────────────────────────────────────────  step 1
        │  await-free: tree, ledger, provenance, _absorbed
        ▼
   observe()  ──→  CampaignObservation  ─────────────────────────  step 2
        │             (built fresh, never stored)
        ├──→  the reasoner's two halves, in-process  ────────────  step 3
        ├──→  prov_sink  ──→  the plane's event list  ───────────  steps 4, 5
        └──→  GET /observe?since=  ──→  another process  ────────  step 6
                  │
   provenance/*.jsonl, jobs/*.jsonl  ─────────────────────────────  step 7
                  │
   what a caller must do today  ──────────────────────────────────  step 8
        │
   Bridge ──→ CampaignDigest ──→ a sink, per absorbed cycle  ─────  step 9

    Step 1 — a run is absorbed, and the window is shut¶

    What ran between admission and this step — each task's command, and where it is recorded — is invocation.md.

    In: ExecutionResults from the backend. Owner: runtime/executor._absorb:578. Out: DesignNodes on the tree, one results provenance record each, and one id appended to _absorbed per lineage (:601).

    The executor is the only writer of campaign state, and every mutating block is free of await. Its own module docstring (runtime/executor.py:1-16) states the rule and the reason: "adding an await inside observe or _absorb would reintroduce torn reads, and they are nearly impossible to diagnose from provenance afterwards." That property is a discipline, not something the language enforces, and it is the single constraint that shapes everything below — an observation cannot be pushed anywhere from inside the code that produces the state it describes. A sink that must be awaited cannot be called here.

    _absorbed (:318) is an append-only list of node ids in absorption order, and _cursor (:319) is how far the last consumer read. Those two integers are the whole of the campaign's notion of "what is new", and step 2 is where they are read.

    Step 2 — observe() assembles the snapshot¶

    In: the executor's own state, plus an optional since watermark. Owner: runtime/executor.observe:540. Out: a CampaignObservation (core/decision.py:75).

    Synchronous, read-only, and the only place in the package that constructs one. It computes the Pareto front over live nodes, builds PopulationStats (core/decision.py:66) inline, and reads recent out of _absorbed[cursor:] (:549) with seq = len(_absorbed) (:550).

    since is the one subtle parameter. Omitted, it reports from _cursor — what the last consumer saw. Given, it reports from that watermark instead, without moving the cursor, so an observer can read the same evidence a policy is about to read without disturbing it. The docstring calls this parity of evidence, and ADR 0007 makes it binding rather than incidental.

    Two declared fields are never populated by this method: failures (core/decision.py:87) and PopulationStats.diversity (:71). And one thing a reader would expect is absent — the parameter values a planner chose. Those reach stages provenance under mappings (runtime/executor.py:1005, written at :1019) and nowhere else, so an observation says what a campaign found and not what it tried.

    Step 3 — the two halves are shown it, whole¶

    In: the observation. Owner: runtime/roles.py:86 (obs = await session.observe(), once at the top of every turn). Out: SpecEvidence (policy/specifier.py:36) and PlanningContext (policy/planner.py:37).

    Both wrappers hold the observation rather than summarising it — SpecEvidence's own docstring says observation is the object observe() returned, not a digest of it. So there is exactly one description of campaign state in the system, and a reasoner, an external caller and a test all read the same fields. That is what makes the control models comparable: a difference in their behaviour is a difference in judgement, not in what they were told.

    What refuses a second shape here: nothing mechanical. The discipline is the import contract — policy may import core and nothing else (tests/test_layering.py), so a half cannot reach the executor to ask a narrower question.

    Step 4 — provenance is mirrored to every listener¶

    In: every provenance record. Owner: runtime/executor._log:356. Out: a write to provenance/<kind>.jsonl, then a guarded call to each registered listener.

    def _log(self, kind: str, record: dict[str, Any]) -> None:
    self.prov.append(kind, record)
    for sink in list(self._prov_listeners):
        try:
            sink(self.spec.campaign_id, kind, dict(record))
        except Exception as broken:
            self.prov_dropped += 1
            log.warning(...)

    This is the only synchronous hook out of a running campaign, which is why a sink that must be awaited cannot be one — that is step 9's job. It was a single slot until recently, and two call sites assigned it without checking for each other: control/plane.py:104 for the event stream and cli/__init__.py:890 for the log heartbeat. Last writer won, in silence, so serving a campaign over HTTP turned off its own progress log. Both now call add_prov_listener, prov_sink remains as a property over the first listener so no caller had to move, and each call is guarded and its failures counted — _log runs on every settlement and every absorption, so a listener that raised was aborting a campaign from inside the record of work already done (finding 3).

    Step 5 — the event stream is that mirror, kept in memory¶

    In: prov_sink calls. Owner: control/plane.InProcessControlPlane.emit:112, into self._events (:94). Out: events() (:143), by cursor page or as a tail.

    _events is a dict[str, list[Event]] in the serving process. It is unbounded, never persisted and lost when the process exits. events(stream=True) polls its own list every 50 ms, and the SSE layer polls that at 100 ms (control/http.py:151). The method's own docstring is honest about it: it polls "because provenance is appended to rather than published. A push-based source would replace the body of this loop and nothing else."

    What refuses an addition here: ADR 0007 — no adapter may add an operation absent from the core protocol. A new way to be told would be a change to CampaignControlPlane, not an adapter's business.

    Step 6 — the observation crosses a process boundary intact¶

    In: GET /campaigns/<cid>/observe?since=. Owner: control/http.py:200 server side, ControlPlaneClient.observe:304 client side. Out: the same CampaignObservation, rebuilt from model_dump(mode="json").

    This already works and is exercised end to end. It is the only machine-readable, serialisable description of campaign state the system has, and anything that wants to tell a caller something should start from it rather than invent a second vocabulary.

    Its cost is the other half of the fact. The payload embeds the whole ToolkitView — every TaskCard, and each toolkit's full SKILL.md text (tools/view.view_of:67) — plus the applied Spec and every DesignNode on the front, rebuilt on every call, with no projection and no field selector. ToolkitView.skills is read by no prompt and no consumer (resolution.md finding 6), so the largest part of what crosses the wire is read by nothing at either end.

    Step 7 — what is actually durable¶

    In: records from _log and the two ledgers. Owner: core/provenance.append:24, exec/ledger.py, exec/task_ledger.py. Out: append-only JSONL under the campaign root.

    file holds
    provenance/campaign.jsonl the spec, goal, objectives and budget, once at start
    provenance/specs.jsonl each Spec generation and its rationale
    provenance/graphs.jsonl each composed graph, its signature, trust and estimate
    provenance/stages.jsonl the deployed Stage — the only record of chosen parameters
    provenance/executions.jsonl settlement and run-finished events
    provenance/results.jsonl one row per absorbed node: QC verdict, failed gates, metrics
    provenance/tasks.jsonl per task, including cost_estimated and cost_measured
    provenance/decisions.jsonl the reasoner's transcript
    provenance/transitions.jsonl promotions, demotions, stops, losses
    jobs/ledger.jsonl, jobs/tasks.jsonl run and task state, folded on read

    append opens, writes and closes per record, so a record is durable when the call returns. The log is truth; checkpoints are an index.

    There is no observations.jsonl. An observation is computed on demand and discarded, so cycle N's view of the campaign cannot be read back — it has to be re-derived from graphs, results, executions and specs. Nothing today needs to replay one, which is why this is recorded as a property rather than fixed.

    Step 8 — so what must a caller do today¶

    In: a wish to be told. Out: one of three unsatisfying options.

    option how what it costs
    Assign prov_sink mgr.prov_sink = fn, as cli/__init__.py:890 does for the log heartbeat one slot, synchronous, unguarded; the caller must reconstruct cycles from provenance records
    Run impress-a serve and poll serve_campaign:459, then GET /observe a second process and a socket, and the whole ToolkitView on every poll
    Hold the manager and schedule a task mgr.observe() through the _DELEGATED allowlist (manager.py:46) the caller must write the pump, and nothing tells it when something landed

    The third is the one that works, and the precedent for it is already in the tree. cli._heartbeat:758 does exactly this for logging, and its docstring states the licence: "Reads only the executor's synchronous snapshot API. Its mutating blocks are await-free by invariant, so a heartbeat scheduled between them can never see a torn state." A pump that awaits a sink outside the executor is a heartbeat with a sink instead of a logger, and it needs nothing added to the executor at all.

    Step 9 — the bridge, which is that pump with a sink¶

    In: a CampaignManager and a CycleSink. Owner: integrations/reporting.Bridge. Out: one CampaignDigest per absorbed cycle, and reports/<sink>.jsonl beside provenance.

    run_with_sink(manager, sink) races the campaign against the pump, exactly as cli.run_campaign already races it against the heartbeat. The executor is unchanged: the pump reads observe() between its mutating blocks and awaits the sink outside them, so no await enters a block that writes campaign state.

    decision why
    Triggered on obs.seq advancing the absorption watermark. Not cycle, which is incremented in _dispatch:1036 and so counts submissions — a report fired there carries no results. Not _activity, which pump:1471 clears, so a second consumer would race it. A monotone integer is race-free.
    period_s is a floor, not the only trigger a cycle of the real chain runs minutes of tasks with nothing absorbed, and a caller's staleness bound has to be satisfiable while that happens
    One report before the run, one after the first is a liveness anchor before any work exists to describe; the last is the only one guaranteed to be read, because the stores a caller receives these through keep one value per workflow — so no digest may be a delta
    Every sink call guarded a sink is somebody else's code reaching into a process holding an allocation. A listener that raises costs a log line and a counter, never the run — the rule policy/reasoning.py:175 already applies, and the one _log still does not (finding 3)
    Digests land in reports/, not provenance/ the executor is the only writer there, and a tenth provenance kind would make a record of what somebody else was told look like a record of what the campaign did

    CampaignDigest (core/decision.py) is a projection of observe()'s return value, not a new operation, so ADR 0007 stays out of play for the same reason adding a field to the observation does. It drops the ToolkitView, the Spec and the DesignNode bodies — static for the campaign, and already given to a caller once by impress-a offer — and carries both spending scales under distinct names, because declared and measured are two different numbers and a caller charging its own budget wants the second (finding 8).

    Pause is in the same loop, because it is the same cadence. It gates admission: in-flight runs are allowed to finish, since cancellation is advisory, so a paused campaign keeps spending for up to one cycle.

    What the observation plane does not enforce¶

    Eleven places where the implementation does not do what a reader of the design would reasonably assume, two of them since repaired and marked in place rather than deleted, because the evidence that forced them is the useful part. Each is verified against the source at the anchor given. Read this section before changing any step above.

    # the reasonable assumption what the code does anchor item
    1 An observation reports what went wrong, and how diverse the population is failures and PopulationStats.diversity are declared and never populated by the only method that builds one core/decision.py:87,71, runtime/executor.py:536-562 G
    2 A campaign's own view of itself at cycle N can be read back No observation is ever persisted. Nine provenance kinds, none of them observations; cycle N's view must be re-derived from four other files core/provenance.py:22, runtime/executor.py:484 G
    3 A campaign can be watched by more than one listener RESOLVED. It could not: prov_sink was one slot, and control/plane.py:104 and cli/__init__.py:890 both assigned it, so attaching a monitor evicted the log heartbeat silently — and the call was unguarded, so a listener that raised stopped the campaign from inside a provenance write, the most expensive place to raise. It is a guarded list now, with prov_dropped counting failures so a deaf subscriber is discoverable, and prov_sink kept as a property over the first listener. The rule is policy/reasoning.py:175's, arriving late runtime/executor.py:343-365 G18
    4 The event stream is a stream _events is an unbounded in-memory list, never persisted, lost on exit; "streaming" is a 50 ms poll of that list with a 100 ms poll on top control/plane.py:94,160-167, control/http.py:151 G
    5 An observation says what the campaign tried, not only what it found Chosen parameter values are absent. They reach stages provenance under mappings and no observation field, so a caller sees scores without the knobs that produced them runtime/executor.py:1005,1019 G, and resolution.md finding 7's neighbour
    6 The payload carries what its consumers read It embeds every TaskCard and each toolkit's whole SKILL.md, plus the applied Spec and the full front, rebuilt per call with no projection — and ToolkitView.skills is read by nothing at either end tools/view.py:67, runtime/executor.py:533-562 G
    7 Watching a campaign is free of consequence observe() refreshes the liveness clock. touch() sets _last_contact, and _control_lost_for is what drains and stops a campaign whose caller vanished. The coupling is deliberate — the Listener polls observe() every cycle, so no heartbeat operation was added — but the consequence is that a purely passive observer keeps drain-and-stop from ever firing, which is exactly the shape of a caller that watches without steering runtime/executor.py:532,491-498,501-525 G
    8 Spending reported to a caller is spending RESOLVED. It was not: BudgetLedger.spent is declared cost - _settle settles results.cost, documented "DECLARED, summed - unchanged on purpose" - and declared figures sit multiples above measurement deliberately, because they feed gates that refuse graphs. So an outer manager told our spend was told the headroom too, and its own learned cost could never converge. _settle now also calls charge_measured(results.cost_measured), which was already summed per run and folded into nothing, and CampaignDigest reports both scales under distinct names. The measured figure bounds nothing and gates nothing, deliberately: a measurement arrives after the work it describes is paid for, so an admission gate reading it would refuse tomorrow's work on yesterday's luck runtime/executor.py:1170-1175, core/budget.py:84-91 G20
    9 A TaskCard carries the cost terms its spec declares RESOLVED. It did not: tools/view.py:46-48 passed burn_rate= and max_commitment= to a model that declared neither field, and pydantic's default extra is ignore, so both were discarded in silence while core/budget.py:67 documented the rule min(goal.budget[d], card.max_commitment[d]) against a field that could not exist. Both are fields now. The fix is not extra="forbid" — these models cross the HTTP boundary, so forbidding extras would turn a newer peer's observation into a 500 — so the general case is guarded mechanically instead, by tests/test_layering.py::test_every_keyword_passed_to_a_core_model_is_a_field_of_it tools/view.py:46-48, core/toolkit.py:59-89 D1, realised in our own code rather than in a user's YAML
    10 A caller can project a burn-down from what it is told There is no rate. BudgetLedger is constructed without walltime=, so burn_rate() returns empty permanently and project() is inert. Limits, spent and holds cross; a rate does not runtime/executor.py:230, core/budget.py:160,172 E
    11 The chain that runs is the chain the campaign declared _admit_once composes against the whole Registry and reports available: self.reg.ids(); nothing restricts a submitted Stage to spec.stages. The bound holds by construction for the shipped planners, whose ToolkitView is narrowed — it is not a gate, and anything pricing a campaign from stages is relying on that runtime/executor.py:673-694, tools/view.py:55 G, and resolution.md's "available means three different things"

    One asymmetry that is by design and is listed so it is not mistaken for the above. Gate 1 does not check that a required root port was satisfied — the loop is a literal pass commented "roots may be satisfied from campaign inputs" (compose/validate.py:81) — because input data arrives through params: rather than as typed reference data. That is resolution.md finding 1, and it belongs to resolution rather than to observation; it appears here only because a caller reading unsatisfied_root_ports off an offer is reading a computation, not a gate.

    Seeing it on disk¶

    Every step's output except step 2's is in provenance, which is the only way to check this page rather than trust it. impress-a mock-lab --only arms --stage sweep writes one campaign per arm in about half a minute with no allocation.

    step where it landed
    1 provenance/results.jsonl — one row per absorbed node
    2 nowhere — finding 2
    3 provenance/decisions.jsonl — what each half was asked and answered
    4, 5 provenance/*.jsonl is the record; the event list is memory only
    6 the campaign's control_plane.url, written 0600 under serve
    7 the table in step 7
    8 the heartbeat +… lines in a run log, which is step 8's third option already working
    9 reports/<sink>.jsonl under the campaign root — one line per digest, with the ts it was sent
    — telemetry/ under the campaign root, when telemetry_checkpoint_s > 0: the engine's own trace, a span per Stage block with its node tasks and tool commands under it, each naming the backend it ran on. Not one of the nine steps — it is the execution layer's record, not the campaign's

    A step whose output cannot be pointed at in one of those files is a step this page has described wrongly — step 2 included, and that is finding 2 rather than an exception.

    Being one workflow of a larger campaign¶

    The two steps above are the whole of what an enclosing campaign manager needs, and integrations/aucam.py is the hundred lines that connect them to one. It is the only module in the package that imports AuCaM, which tests/test_layering.py asserts, so the dependency stays optional and one file wide.

    from impress_a.integrations.aucam import launch_bounds, reward_fn, workflow_config

config = workflow_config("campaigns/delta-small-molecule.yaml", model="D")
# -> an AuCaM WorkflowConfig: declared_spending and spending_limit from the offer,
#    resources from its resource shape, supports_pause=True, stale_timeout_s at 4x
#    the report interval the offer declares.
bounds = launch_bounds(offer)          # for the caller's own LaunchRequest.bounds
policy = BanditSchedulingPolicy(reward_fn=reward_fn)

    Four things in that adapter are decisions rather than plumbing, and each is a defect avoided:

    • It does not declare flow. AuCaM passes its shared engine to a block that asks for one. Taking it would be wrong twice: WorkflowEngine.__init__ captures the loop it is built on, and the executor must build its own through exec.backend.make_engine_bounded, which also bounds Dragon's synchronous startup. There is no "use this engine instead" seam, and inventing one is how jobs 22328172 and 22328262 spent their allocations on an engine that dispatched nothing. The price is that our tasks are invisible to the caller's per-task telemetry — which is why spending travels in our own reports.
    • The caller's spending limit becomes the campaign's own budget, narrowing it and never loosening it. A limit enforced from outside can only cancel a campaign after it has overspent; the same number as a budget is refused by reserve and gate 5 before a task runs, and the campaign stops itself with a reason naming the dimension.
    • score is a float, not a dict. AuCaM's default bandit reward is 1.0 if returncode == 0 else 0.0 — throughput, and its own comment says science scores belong to the controller — so no shipped policy of theirs can rank a dict. The scalar is progress toward the asked-for front; reward_fn is what ranks arms by it.
    • metrics carries the MEASURED spend under the keys the caller charges from. Without them it prorates our declared cost, and the declared figures are inflated on purpose (finding 8).

    The campaign is still ours throughout: it keeps the engine, the outer loop and the decision to stop, and the caller gets no privilege over campaign state that a policy would not have. That is decision 0014, and tests/test_aucam.py runs a real AuCaM campaign over a real IMPRESS-A one to prove the page is describing something that works.
