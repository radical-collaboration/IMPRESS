    The mock knob lab¶

    A measuring harness, not a result. It runs the laptop surrogate of the small-molecule binding chain across a grid of knob settings, flattens what the executor wrote into four CSVs, and draws six figures from them. The whole thing finishes in about half a minute on a laptop and needs no allocation.

    Read campaigns/README.md first for what the surrogate campaign is and what each knob does. This page is about the harness around it: what it runs, what it measures, and which of its numbers mean anything.

    To drive it rather than read about it, Ten minutes with a mock campaign runs the four arms in Colab and walks the two traps below — the G14 stop-reason gap and the decision-0013 trust split — as a predict-then-run exercise.

    What a figure from here is allowed to claim¶

    Every metric the six surrogates report is a seeded random draw (toolkits/mock/SKILL.md). A response surface drawn here is a statement about the surrogate's own arithmetic. It is not evidence about any molecule, and the disclaimer is printed on every figure from analysis/mock_lab.DISCLAIMER rather than typed into a caption a reader could drop when they paste the PNG into a slide.

    What is measured honestly is the campaign's behaviour — how many cycles it took, which gate refused what, whether the Pareto front filled, how long the control plane spent deciding — because that is the same executor, the same composer, the same five gates and the same interlock the Delta arms run. A behaviour observed here is the behaviour the real campaign would show; a number observed here is not a measurement.

    Running it¶

    impress-a mock-lab                                   # sweep, collect, figures
impress-a mock-lab --only arms --stage sweep         # the four arms, nothing else
impress-a mock-lab --stage figures                   # redraw from the tables already there
impress-a mock-lab --repeats 6 --jobs 8              # a tighter band, same wall clock

    Figures need the optional extra: pip install -e ".[figures]". The sweep and collect stages do not — they write CSVs with the standard library, so a machine with no plotting stack can still produce the tables.

    Output lands in campaigns/_runs/_analysis by default, which is gitignored:

    manifest.json          every cell, its knob setting, its exit code, its run directory
specs/<cell>.yaml      the campaign that cell actually ran
runs/<cell>/           that campaign's own run root - provenance, ledger, trust
logs/<cell>.log        its stdout and stderr tail
tables/*.csv           lineages, tasks, cycles, campaigns, phases
figures/*.png          the six figures

    The two kinds of cell¶

    Four arms, which are the settings the ten-minute activity walks through and the four hues in analysis/plot_style.py. Each is one reproducible campaign a person can re-run by hand and get back.

    They are named for what the campaign DOES, not for what the surrogate reports. Every mock metric is a seeded random draw, so a name like "low-plddt" would be naming noise, while "aborted after three all-failed runs" is a control-plane outcome that reproduces. The tier each arm takes in the palette follows the same rule and is assigned once, in analysis/plot_style.py, never per figure.

    Arm Setting What the campaign does
    baseline shipped defaults clears every bar; the Spec is satisfied; the front fills
    rescued difficulty: 0.55, plus diffusion_steps: 200, packmin.cycles: 5, diffusion_samples: 25 the same difficulty, bought back by three knobs
    hard difficulty: 0.55 acceptance shortfall; the front stays empty; trust untouched
    hopeless difficulty: 0.85 integrity failure; CampaignAborted after three all-failed runs

    Two things the table cannot say. hard and rescued are a matched pair at identical difficulty, differing only in the three knobs raised to buy it back — which is what separates "can knobs recover this campaign?" from "is this campaign hard?". Under a one-dial sweep those two questions are the same column, which is why the pair exists as arms rather than as sweep points.

    One caveat on rescued, measured from its own stages.jsonl rather than assumed: two of its three knobs only hold for cycle 1. Exploring emits no parameters, so the arm's values stand; from cycle 2 the planner escalates diffusion_steps and packmin.cycles from the tool spec's defaults (50 and 1) and the policy wins the merge, so they become 88 and 2 — below what the arm declared. diffusion_samples survives at 25 because it is a breadth knob no planner may touch. The campaign is real and the measured behaviour is real; what the label overstates is how much of it is held across cycles. Backlog G17, which is where the fix is being argued.

    And hard versus hopeless is the acceptance/integrity line, not a matter of degree. Between roughly 0.5 and 0.75 the dial only misses the 0.5 acceptance threshold: the node FAILs and the pattern's trust is untouched. Above about 0.75 it drives ss_fraction under the 0.3 has_secondary_structure floor, which is an integrity failure — the node FAILs and the pattern is demoted (decision 0013). Reaching for the dial to "make the campaign fail" without noticing which kind of failure you bought is the mistake the mock toolkit exists to make visible.

    Sweep cells, one campaign per knob value per repeat, each repeat under a different seed salt. These carry the ensembles: 8 lineages a cell times 3 repeats is 24 independent draws per point at the default settings.

    Two deliberate differences from the shipped campaign, applied to every cell equally:

    • replicas: 4 (arms) or 8 (sweeps), because one lineage per cycle is not a distribution. This changes the pattern signature, so every cell runs untrusted under the interlock's 10% cost cap — the same interlock for all of them.
    • A sweep cell runs exactly one cycle. ThresholdSpecifier.revise adds a refinement objective as soon as the front is non-empty, so a multi-cycle cell mixes the knob's response with the planner's escalation away from it, and a sweep that cannot separate those is not a response surface.

    Isolation, and why it is not tidiness¶

    Each cell runs as a subprocess of impress-a run with its own IMPRESS_A_RUN_ROOT, IMPRESS_A_TRUST_DIR and IMPRESS_A_SEED_SALT.

    The trust directory is the one that bites. The trust ledger is cross-run state: a cell that promoted a pattern changes the interlock every later cell runs under, so a shared ledger makes the sweep a function of its own iteration order rather than of the knob. The salt is what makes --repeat draw different lineages instead of the same ones again (backlog G7).

    The four arms always run one at a time, whatever --jobs says, because every wall-clock figure comes from them. Run eight-wide, one arm's first cycle came back at 0.10s against 1.10s for the other three — scheduling noise presented as a property of the knob setting. Sweep cells are read only for the metrics their tools drew, which CPU contention cannot change, so they run in parallel.

    The reasoner axis¶

    The six surrogates mock the tools. --reasoners mocks nothing: it varies the control model above them, so the four knob arms run under each named reasoner and the lab measures the half the four-model taxonomy is actually about.

    impress-a mock-lab --reasoners D --reasoners null                    # offline, free
impress-a mock-lab --reasoners D --reasoners B --model-profile beam  # live
    letter what drives the campaign needs a profile repeated
    D ThresholdSpecifier + ThresholdPlanner; rules end to end no no
    null an empty Spec, so the campaign stops at cycle 0 no no
    A the four-node graph; two nodes call the model (hypothesize, select_tasks) yes --reasoner-repeats, default 3
    B two schemas, one call each (SPEC_SCHEMA, STAGE_SCHEMA) yes --reasoner-repeats

    C is absent: it needs impress-a serve plus an impress-a control sidecar waiting on the 0600 control_plane.url, which is two processes per cell, and its caller-side reasoner records have nowhere to land (backlog H8).

    Sweeps stay on D whatever the axis says. They measure the surrogate's own response surface, which no reasoner changes, so running them under four models buys four readings of one piece of arithmetic.

    Three rules the live path enforces¶

    A live model with no profile is refused, not quietly run. --model A without one runs its rule cascades and --model B runs RehearsalOracle. Both report zero LLM calls — which is exactly what a degraded live arm reports. A figure labelled "model B" drawn from the rehearsal is the pre-registration's §8 void outcome manufactured on purpose, so the lab exits 2 and names the models it refused, before spending anything.

    One probe call before any live cell. cli._client_for opens no socket, so without this the first failure lands inside a campaign cycle, where reasoning.ask retries twice, writes reasoner_degraded and runs model D's cascade — and the campaign exits 0. Every live cell would do the same and the lab would draw a full set of figures for a model that never answered. One round trip up front is the difference between "the endpoint is down" and twenty-four quietly degraded arms.

    A cap on what it may spend. --max-llm-calls (default 400), counted from what each cell actually recorded rather than estimated — a corrective retry is a second call and a degraded node is two calls that bought nothing. A cell the cap stopped is named in the manifest and carries a skipped reason, because one silently missing from a figure reads as a model that had nothing to show.

    What the lab reads about a reasoner¶

    Nothing recorded a control model's behaviour until 890829d: ReasonerOptions.sink had writers throughout policy/ and no subscriber, so a campaign left no prompt, no reply, no model id, no timing and no degraded event. Two of the collected tables exist because of that fix.

    • decisions.csv — one row per LLM call: node, attempt, whether it was accepted, the model_id that answered, elapsed_s, and prompt/completion tokens as deltas. The prompt and reply bodies stay in decisions.jsonl; a CSV is for counting.
    • params.csv — the parameter vector each Planner chose, from stages.jsonl's mappings. source separates campaign from reasoner, because mappings holds the effective vector and _apply_campaign_defaults merges the campaign's params: into it before composition. Without the split every model would read as having set every knob.
    • campaigns.csv gains llm_calls, llm_retries, llm_s, llm_share, token totals, s_per_llm_call, degraded_events, and model_id — read from the records rather than from the flag the lab passed, so a cell that asked for beam and was answered by something else shows it.

    Check degraded_events before reading any live arm's yield. A non-zero count means the arm fell back to model D's cascade, and what it produced is not that model's result. That is the pre-registration's void rule, enforceable for the first time now that anything writes these events.

    What the live runs found¶

    Beam-501B-A23B via profile beam, 2026-11-10: model B alone (20 calls), then D + A + B at one repeat (31), then three repeats of every live cell (89 calls, 28 cells), which is the run the figures are drawn from. Zero retries and zero degraded turns in all three, and no allocation.

    model D model A (live) model B (live)
    seconds per decision 0.33 9.23 19.36
    seconds per call — 2.8 7.2
    completion tokens per call — 386 1110
    share of campaign wall clock at the endpoint — 84–99% 93–99%
    cycles run 2–4 1–1 1–6
    knobs it moved 3, all quality 4, all quality 9, mostly breadth
    front on hard, over 3 repeats 0 of 12 3–4 of 4 0 of 16
    predicted GPU-h per campaign 1.4–2.0 0.68, flat 0.68–4.1

    A live model is not a function, and three repeats is the smallest number that shows it. Model B ran the rescued arm as one cycle with an empty front twice and as six cycles with four designs on the front once — same arm, same salt, same endpoint. Model A was identical in all three. Any single-repeat figure here would have been a draw reported as a property; the repeats are what let f7 and f11 draw a range instead of a bar.

    A and B differ in what they are shown, not only in what they choose. Model A's parameter vector comes from parameterize, a deterministic node the model steers only by naming a strategy, and _knobs.tunable keeps breadth parameters away from it. Model B is handed the whole task card and picks for itself. So A raising four quality dials and B raising num_designs is a comparison of a filtered knob set against an unfiltered one.

    Two things worth carrying off the laptop. The endpoint is the campaign on this tier: the six tools return in milliseconds and 94–99% of each live cell's wall clock was one model thinking. And model B raises breadth parameters that models A and D are forbidden to touch — num_designs to 2, 3, 4 and 8 — because OraclePlanner._prompt filters what it shows by tunable, which does not look at kind. rfd3_design's own spec records that num_designs stays at 1 because its adapter keeps index 0, and its cost_model is per invocation, so nothing prices the extra draws either. Backlog H10.

    And a live Specifier can skip the abort rule. On hopeless — difficulty 0.85, the arm whose backbone breaks — model A stopped at cycle 1 on "every spec objective satisfied" with all four lineages failing the ss_fraction integrity gate and an empty front, where the deterministic control ran on and aborted on three consecutive all-failed runs. Its Specifier emitted no refinement objective, so nothing was left unsatisfied and a third cycle never came. The quality dials it had raised made the numbers good on a structure difficulty 0.85 had already destroyed — which is visible in f10, where that arm clears every band but ss_fraction.

    Over three repeats it is A 3 of 3 and B 1 of 3, so it is not a model-A property: it is what any live Specifier does when it declines to add a refinement objective, and B does that one time in three. Backlog H11 — G14's mechanism with a safety consequence rather than a reporting one.

    difficulty is operator_only¶

    OraclePlanner shows a live model every tunable parameter with its range and its trades sentence, and _apply_campaign_defaults states that a policy wins on any key it set. The dial reads "how hard this campaign is", 0.0 to 1.0. A model asked to satisfy the objectives turns it down, the hard arm quietly becomes the easy one, and nothing says so.

    So it is marked operator_only in mock_rfd3_design/spec.yaml: the campaign may declare it, a reasoner may not propose it. Enforced in one seam, tools/view.card_for, which reports it under TaskCard.frozen and leaves it out of TaskCard.parameters — _knobs.tunable skips it, B's prompt omits it, and B's schema check refuses a reply naming it with the reason the spec gave. Gate 3 reads ToolSpec.frozen_parameters, which stays empty, so the campaign path is untouched and no seeded draw moves.

    Checked rather than assumed: campaigns.csv carries difficulty_proposed, which is empty unless a reasoner's value reached a deployed stage or a rejection named the dial as frozen, and f9-knobs-moved.png asserts that it appears for nobody.

    The tables¶

    File One row per Carries
    lineages.csv DesignNode the whole chain's metrics merged, the QC verdict, and the failed gates split into failed_integrity / failed_acceptance
    tasks.csv task its stage_index in the chain, verdict, gates, timing, both sides of its cost, and every metric it reported prefixed m_
    cycles.csv composed graph signature, whether it was trusted, the interlock's scrutiny, the estimate, and the gate that refused it if one did
    campaigns.csv cell stop reason, whether it aborted, cycles, front size, verdict counts, wall clock, busy time, overhead, decisions and tasks per second
    phases.csv cycle where that cycle's wall clock went, segment by segment

    Two things in there are easy to get subtly wrong, so they are done once and tested:

    • Busy time is a union of intervals, not a sum of durations. workers: 4 overlaps tasks, so a sum double-counts; and wall - sum can go negative and then reads as negative overhead. The union is correct at any concurrency.
    • A metric a tool did not report is None, never 0.0. A tool omits a metric it could not read (the metrics_reported invariant), and fabricating a zero turns "the tool broke" into "the design is weak".

    f10 and f11: the comparison as a live campaign will make it¶

    analysis/compare_arms.py scores a Delta arm on two axes, and these two draw the same axes on the surrogate — with the two places the surrogate cannot follow stated rather than papered over.

    f10, the protein axis. The seven metrics tiers.T2_THRESHOLDS and T3_THRESHOLDS test, read at import rather than retyped, one panel each, a mark per arm per model and the band shaded on the side that clears. What it does not draw is a tier count: the live headline is distinct_t2/distinct_t3, which is tiers after clustering at 90 % sequence identity and 2.5 Å Cα RMSD, and the surrogate emits neither a sequence nor a structure. A count here would be an upper bound wearing a measured number's name. The yield comparison stays in f7, where the denominator is the Pareto front and nothing is implied about distinctness.

    f11, the cost axis. compare_arms charges an arm AllocNodes × gpus_per_node × Elapsed_h from sacct. There is no allocation here, and the laptop's clock is not a stand-in — 82–99 % of it is one model thinking, which inverts completely on hardware. What does transfer is the decision: which tools ran and how many times. Charged at the real toolkit's declared rates (rfd3 0.1 GPU-h, ligandmpnn 0.02, boltz 0.05, fastrelax 0.25 CPU-h), that is the same arithmetic gate 5 and the untrusted-pattern cap do before admitting a graph. Every number on it is labelled predicted. The mock specs' own cost models are deliberately uniform at 0.002 and carry no information, so they are not used.

    It also shows H10 without having to argue it: models A and B cost the same predicted GPU-hours in every arm, because the estimate is per invocation and B's num_designs: 4 does not enter it.

    f3, f4 and f5 are drawn for one control model. They pool by arm, and an arm stops being one campaign once the reasoner axis exists: averaging D's 0.14s decision with B's 20.5s one under the arm's hue measures the endpoint, not the knob. When a lab has more than one model these three keep the deterministic control — D, or the first model on the axis if D is not on it — and say so in the caption. A single-model lab is unaffected, caption included.

    The control model does not get a hue. The four validated colours are spent on the four knob arms and colour follows the entity, so in f7–f9 a model takes the fill channel — hatch and face alpha inside the arm's own colour (plot_style.MODEL_FILLS). It survives every CVD simulation by construction, reads in greyscale, and the arm identity a reader learned in f1–f6 carries straight across.

    Colour follows analysis/plot_style.py and means the same thing in all nine: blue is the best-performing configuration, magenta is the run that did not complete, and a sweep line is drawn in the neutral grey because a sweep is a measure rather than one of the compared entities. Bands are ± 2σ population spread over lineages, never a standard error — at 24 lineages the two differ by about five times, and a band read as a confidence interval makes adjacent knob settings look separated when they are not.

    Three things the lab found¶

    Worth knowing before you read its output, because two of them are defects in the system it measures rather than in the surrogate.

    1. hard stops on "every spec objective satisfied" with an empty Pareto front. Not a bug in the lab. DataRegistry.ingest records a task's metrics whatever the QC verdict and SatisfactionRule counts those readings, while core/pareto correctly refuses a FAIL node a place on the front. f6-stop-vs-front.png exists to make that gap unmissable. Backlog G14.
    2. An aborted campaign's provenance says reason: completed. hopeless raises CampaignAborted and the only durable trace of why is the process's stderr, so collect derives aborted from the exit code instead. Backlog G15.
    3. About one first cycle in eight stalls for an extra ~1.0s inside asyncflow's run-component loop, which waits on its component-change event with a 1.0s timeout. Irrelevant on hardware, 90% of the first cycle here, and the reason every wall-clock caption says so. Backlog G16.

    Extending it¶

    Add a knob to SWEEPS in analysis/mock_lab.py — the tool, the parameter and the values — and it sweeps. Add an arm to ARMS and it must also get a hue in plot_style.RUN_STYLES; style_for raises on an unknown arm rather than inventing one, because a silent fifth hue would break the colour identity every figure in a report depends on. A fifth categorical colour has to be re-validated first — see the campaign-plot-style skill's references/palette.md.
